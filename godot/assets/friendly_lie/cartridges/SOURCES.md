# Vintage slot-cartridge templates

Generated with OpenAI image generation on 2026-11-17 for the designer's request for heavy 1950s card carriers, external BW tabs, type-specific constructions and restrained rarity differences.

- `script.png`: stamped nickel/steel and Bakelite, exposed cloth-insulated wiring, ceramic resistors and fuse.
- `program.png`: cast-aluminum case, metal-can transistor, wire loop and glass vacuum tube.
- `protocol.png`: celadon-enamel relay carrier, terminal block, resistor bank and exposed wiring.

- `bloatware.png`: Whirlwind-inspired protective handle, paired tubes and open cage, with an oxide-red phenolic board face.

The PNGs are empty template illustrations with transparency. Live Godot controls supply names, costs, type/tags, authored rarity, card rules and art. The authored types remain SCRIPT/PROGRAM/PROTOCOL/BLOATWARE; rarity data and reward odds are unchanged. Common/start-of-run carriers use plain finishes; uncommon/rare indexing and metal details are rendered by code, with full rarity labels for non-color identification. Bloatware uses the oxide-red Whirlwind cartridge selected in `744f3dc` and recorded in the master design document. The designer approved the cartridge set and its finishes in VISUAL-APPROVALS-002.

The 2026-11-17 face revision uses generated edits of the same three frames to replace beige label material with light ivory SCRIPT stock, pearl PROGRAM plate and pale celadon PROTOCOL enamel. Titles are live dark printing with no black bar; no title text is baked into the assets.


Selected board-material revision (2026-11-17): generated edits use A-family tobacco/khaki brown for SCRIPT (IBM SMS material photo), C-family olive for PROGRAM (GE-225 board photo) and D-family blue-green for PROTOCOL (RCA connector material photo). These references include early-1960s examples; they guide material colour rather than a claim of exact 1950s manufacture. Whirlwind construction reference: Computer History Museum object 102732036, https://www.computerhistory.org/collections/catalog/102732036. The generated `bloatware.png` adapts its protective handle, paired tubes and open cage to the slot-cartridge format. The designer selected a distinct oxide-red board face for Bloatware; light plate/brown Bloat prototypes were rejected and are superseded. No museum photograph is used directly as game art. Labels and rules remain live controls.
