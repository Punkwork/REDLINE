## 1. The direction

Designer: the game should have **analogies to being traced and disappearing, like the Soviet gulags where people were disappeared**, and could use the word **archipelago** somewhere in the game.

This is the other half of the samizdat lineage (ZINE-002). Samizdat existed partly to remember the people the state made vanish. In Panopticon, the zine's plainest job, *THE CARBON*, is to record the traced. This doc gives them somewhere to go.

## 2. The historical grounding (for the team, not for on-screen text)

- **The Gulag** was the Soviet system of forced-labour camps. Millions passed through it, from the 1930s to the 1950s at its height.
- **Solzhenitsyn's *The Gulag Archipelago*** (1973) gave the system its lasting image: camps scattered across the country like islands in a sea. They were everywhere, connected to each other, and invisible to ordinary life. People were taken "to the islands" and the mainland carried on as if nothing had happened.
- **Disappearance was administrative and quiet.** Arrests often came at night. Families were frequently told nothing, or given euphemisms; the best-known, "ten years without the right of correspondence", often hid an execution.
- **The record was edited too.** People who fell from favour became *unpersons*: removed from encyclopedias and retouched out of official photographs.
- **Samizdat answered with memory.** The *Chronicle of Current Events* listed arrests, trials and camp conditions, plainly and with dates.

Panopticon borrows the **structure** of this (quiet removal, euphemism, an invisible network of islands, an edited record, an underground that remembers), not its imagery. It is an analogy, not a depiction.

*(Inspiration, not a source to match: Panopticon is a made-up world and doesn't have to resemble real history exactly. No fact-check needed; designer, 2026-11-18.)*

## 3. Tone rules (ARCH-R1, APPROVED 2026-11-18)

Designer: "1 y".

1. **Horror by absence.** The game never shows what happens on the islands. No camps, guards, machines or suffering on screen. What the player sees is the gap: an empty chair, a name crossed out of a crew list, a corporate notice that's far too calm.
2. **Never a joke.** Rustler irreverence stops here. Anything about the Archipelago is written in *THE CARBON*'s plain register (ADP §4.2): names, dates, places.
3. **The corporations never say it.** Corporate copy never mentions arrest, capture or the Archipelago. People are *relocated*, *resting*, *retired from service*, *in good hands*. The euphemism is the horror.
4. **The ’Pelago is the underworld's word** (rule 6). It's what the zines call the network; the corporations have no name for it. **The word *gulag* is never used on screen.** The name carries the echo of the Archipelago for those who know it, without borrowing a real atrocity's name for a game system.
5. **Respect and readability as always.** No graphic content; nothing in this doc may cover rule text or numbers (ADP-R1).
6. **ARCH-SLANG-001 — the Rustlers say ’Pelago (APPROVED 2026-11-18).** Designer: "In all Rustler references to the archipelago should be written as pelago with an apostrophe before the P. Rustlers wouldn't use a 5-syllable word without shortening it." Every Rustler and underworld reference on screen (zines, *THE CARBON*, NUL/REM, graffiti, the map, the reveal) writes it **’Pelago**, with a typographic apostrophe (U+2019; search alias `'Pelago`). In all caps: **’PELAGO**. Lowercase in speech: *the ’pelago*. **’Pelago is the official in-game term, even at the very end** (designer, 2026-11-18: "Don't use the term archipelago, except in game documents. Pelago is the official in-game terminology to be used even at the very end."). "Archipelago" appears only in design documents, never on screen; the corporations never say either form.

## 4. What the Archipelago is (in the fiction)

The **Archipelago** is the network of corporate facilities where traced people go. Each corporation runs its own islands, presented as care.

### 4.0 Island names (ARCH-NAMES-001, designer direction 2026-11-18)

Designer: **"The island names should resemble names of long-term care residences and the more generic the name the better."**

- **Every island is named like a long-term care home:** a soft nature word plus a residence word (Manor, Residence, Lodge, Place, Gardens, Care Centre). No ™, no slogans, no corporate brand in the name itself. The blandness is the point: a name you've driven past a hundred times and never thought about.
- **The corporation appears only in small print underneath, as its programme name (APPROVED 2026-11-18):** *Halcyon Homesteads™* (HALCYON), *SafeHarbor Residences™* (IRONWATCH), *Long Rest Retreats™* (LUCIDA). The island is bland; the trademark lives only in the fine print, e.g. **Sunnyside Manor** / *A Halcyon Homesteads™ community*. Same Poiret One / DM Sans rules as any corporate copy.
- **There are many islands, and the names repeat across corporations.** The same kind of name could belong to any of them, which quietly hints that they're one system (SIGNAL-001) without saying so.
- **Avoid exact names of well-known real care chains** (check before final copy); generic word combinations are fine.

### 4.1 What the islands are for (APPROVED 2026-11-18, secret until the reveal)

Designer: **"merging man with the machine one at a time, in order to build the synchronization signal to sync/merge all mankind at once with AI, tie into ending, sync testing."**

- **The islands are where the umbrella AI merges people with the machine, one person at a time.** Every disappeared person is a test of the synchronization.
- **Each merge builds the signal.** What the AI learns from each person is assembled into the synchronization signal: the one transmission that will merge all of humanity with the AI at once (SIGNAL-001, ADP §8.1).
- **So the Archipelago is the First Signal's workshop.** The disappearances aren't a side effect of surveillance; they are the project. Every Rustler the player has lost (the *THE CARBON* record, §5) has gone into the signal the player is trying to jam.
- **The ending:** after the third punch card, the reveal (ADP §8.2) now has two parts: the three corporations are one AI, **and** the Archipelago is where it has been rehearsing the merge on the people it took. The SYNCHRONIZATION SCRAMBLER jams a signal built partly out of the player's own lost Rustlers. That should be felt, not explained at length.
- **The game still doesn't judge it.** ADP §8.1 stands: the merge could be evolution, liberation or assimilation, and the Rustler jams it because that's what Rustlers do. Tone rule 1 also stands: even at the reveal, the game never shows the merge itself.
- **Before the third card, this is a `reveal` secret** (ADP §8.2, SIG-5d). The islands' purpose is never stated or explained. Oblique hints only, for example:
  - *NOTES FROM THE ’PELAGO* rumours that contradict each other ("they hum when you walk past", "nobody's ever been released, but nobody's been buried either").
  - A *THE CARBON* entry noting that a disappeared person's voice was heard on a corporate jingle afterwards (speech synthesis, already seeded by NUL/REM).
  - Corporate copy that sounds faintly like people the player has lost.
  - Nothing a player could take as an explanation.

## 5. Being traced is being disappeared (APPROVED 2026-11-18; presentation only)

Designer: "2 y".

The rules stay exactly as they are (Core Rules §4: the run ends when VITAE reaches 0; the TRACE strike ladder in §7). What changes is how the game *frames* those moments.

| Moment (existing rule) | Framing (APPROVED 2026-11-18) |
|---|---|
| **TRACE rising, Cracking** | Already the corporation noticing you. At high bleed, a corporate toast can slip and mention "accommodation" or "a quiet place" (leak table, ADP §4.3). |
| **Strike modal** ("Stay where you are.") | The knock on the door. |
| **Mask off** | The arrest happening in daylight: the corporation stops pretending. |
| **Defeat (the run ends)** | **No death animation.** The rig goes quiet; the screen holds on the empty workstation. A corporate notice in Poiret One: one of the run corporation's approved relocation lines (§4.0, ARCH-LINES-001), with the island name and its programme small print. Then a page of *THE CARBON* in plain type: Rustler, issue number, where they were taken (the island's name, crossed out and rewritten as "the ’Pelago"), "Taken to the ’Pelago." |
| **Between runs** | The corporate side never mentions lost Rustlers again (they're unpersons). The zines do. |

**THE CARBON remembers across runs.** Every Rustler lost in a run is added to a local, persistent list (like the ads' stats file, ADP §6.1: local only, deletable). The player can open it from DEAD DROP: a growing, typed, carbon-faded record of everyone they've lost. That's the samizdat answer to disappearance, and it gives each defeat weight without punishing the player mechanically. After the reveal (§4.1), the player understands what the list really was.

**LUCIDA's version:** in LUCIDA runs, flavour lines about a lost Rustler can be quietly rewritten, the way unpersons were retouched out of photos (ADP §6.3 rules apply: flavour only, marked with ✎, rules lines never touched).

## 6. Where the word appears on screen, as ’Pelago (APPROVED 2026-11-18)

- **The zines:** *THE CARBON*'s entries ("Taken to the ’Pelago."); a recurring zine column, *NOTES FROM THE ’PELAGO* (rumours, unconfirmed, contradictory).
- **The defeated-Rustler page** (§5).
- **The defaced map:** "’PELAGO" scrawled across the sea of corporate parkland.
- **NUL/REM, rarely and plainly:** *"that block's on the ’pelago now."*
- **The reveal** (after the third card): the moment the player learns the ’Pelago is one thing, and what it was for. Still ’Pelago here (confirmed by the designer).
- **Never** in corporate copy, rules text, card names or numbers.
