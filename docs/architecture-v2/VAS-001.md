## 1. Visual thesis and fidelity hierarchy
PANOPTICON should look and feel like a **single physical 1950s retro-futurist surveillance/control machine**: a richly layered copper/chrome/Bakelite chassis containing live CRT instruments, advertising-illustrated ICE portraits, mechanical subroutine indicators, illuminated status gauges, and printed collectible cards. It is dense, expressive and legible, not a sparse modern HUD or a set of unrelated floating panels.

**Priority 1 — silhouette/layout:** preserve overall console silhouette, five-region reading hierarchy, two dominant ICE windows and lower card tray.
**Priority 2 — material language:** deep brown/black enamel, copper/bronze/chrome edging, screws, vents, glass, patina, illuminated lamps, shadows.
**Priority 3 — typography and visual hierarchy:** condensed cream all-caps labels, enamel instrument plaques, 1950s cursive HALCYON advertisement, live numerical readouts.
**Priority 4 — illustrated personality:** approachable but menacing corporate mascots, painted mid-century halftone illustration, teal/seafoam ICE windows.
**Priority 5 — micro-effects:** CRT scan/glow, warm bulbs, animated indicator feedback, tasteful surface wear. Do not sacrifice readability or input performance for effects.

**Do not** replace the image's dense layout with a generic dashboard, flat sci-fi panels, neon-only cyberpunk, large blank areas, or card art from an unrelated style.

## 2. Reference inventory — directly observed
- **Top strip (full width):** left PANOPTICON logo and small interface/node line; turn counter; phase display; TRACE meter; HOSTILE REVEAL percentage; right HALCYON HOME advertising plate with illustrated smiling spokesperson.
- **Left instrument column:** small green CRT with antenna/city imagery; vertically stacked VITAE, BANDWIDTH, RAM, SHIELD and PULP INCOMING meters; lower ACTIVE EFFECTS paper notice; another green CRT below.
- **Center/right encounter well:** broad riveted metallic enclosure; LAYER 1 OF 3 label with progress lamps; EXTRACT plaque; two large rounded metal-rimmed teal ICE display windows side-by-side.
- **Left ICE window:** illustrated cheerful TV-headed corporate mascot labeled AUTO-RENEW, class/STR plaque, four vertically aligned subroutine rows, one explanatory speech bubble, bottom HARDENING effect strip.
- **Right ICE window:** illustrated robotic dog WATCHDOG, class/STR plaque, three vertically aligned subroutine rows, negative-space below; DEADLOCK red and SYN violet treatments.
- **Under ICE windows:** long NEXT RESPONSE illuminated sequence rail with three sample entries, arrows, metallic end vents.
- **Bottom:** five overlapping printed cards with art, BW circles, name/rules text and varied color treatments; to their right DECK / DISCARD / BURNED counters; large glowing circular END TURN button.
- **Surface qualities:** beveled chrome and bronze borders, specular highlights, fine halftone grain, screen glass, screws, dark cavities, indicator lamps, distressed paper and cream typography.

**Image-specific text/values are NOT canonical rules:** the illustration shows two ICE, AUTO-RENEW/Watchdog, STR, PULP, DEADLOCK, SYN, cards and 30/30 VITAE etc. The playable slice must display the actual encounter/hand/phase/values returned by current Game; do not invent mechanics, cards, statuses or new HALCYON copy based on the image.

## 4. Proposed layout adaptation policy
**Primary target:** desktop landscape 16:9, preserving reference composition at 1536×864. Current Godot prototype project uses 1600×1000 (8:5) and `canvas_items` stretch/`expand`; changing project resolution is a separate reviewed decision.

**Recommended approach:** authored logical reference space of 1536×864 within a responsive Godot Control tree; use anchors/containers for outer regions, aspect-preserving/scalable artwork for chassis and bezels, dynamic text/controls layered over them. Verify 1600×1000, 1920×1080, 1366×768 and at least one ultrawide viewport. On 8:5 and ultrawide, use controlled margin/ornament extension or measured reflow, not distortion or clipping of gameplay. On smaller sizes prioritize readable card text and subroutine click targets; an optional accessible zoom/focus mode may be proposed, not silently implemented. The portrait/ICE area must adapt to 1, 2 and 3 ICE, using scale, horizontal scrolling or a reviewed composition variant rather than shrinking text below readability.

**Layering:** (1) machine chassis/backplate, (2) bezel/window framing, (3) live component containers and text, (4) dynamic illustrations, (5) selection/focus/drag overlays, (6) feedback/choice modals. Separate art from interactive text. Use nine-slice or tiled edges for variable-sized hardware panels; avoid stretching screw heads or decorative typography.

## 6. Graphics vs native Godot controls
**Raster/vector art assets:** chassis plates, corner bevels, copper/chrome frame segments, paper textures, CRT glass/noise overlays, vent grilles, ornamental screws, ICE portraits, card illustrations, advertising artwork, decorative antenna/city CRT images.

**Native Godot interactive controls:** card cost and rules text, live resource meters/numbers, ICE names/class/STR, subroutine text and statuses, target hitboxes, deck/discard counts, END TURN button, selection/hover/focus, response labels, pending choice modal and tooltips. Decorative buttons can be art-backed but their semantic labels, states and hitboxes remain live.

**Hybrid:** ICE window background and border are art-backed, while subroutine rows are repeated controls; CardView has decorative printed frame plus dynamic art/text; CRT has shader/decorative trace plus live status text. Do not bake a screenshot of gameplay text into an interactive background.

## 8. Motion, lighting and sound policy
- **CRT:** subtle phosphor bloom, low-opacity scanlines, optional gentle sweep; no constant hard flicker or readability loss.
- **Mechanical controls:** tactile press/depress, short lamp pulse and restrained glow; visual state always corresponds to Game state.
- **Card:** hover lift, small tilt, drag ghost, electric-cyan selected edge; preserve focus/keyboard equivalence.
- **ICE:** animated target highlight, break feedback and response flash; effects cannot obscure labels or delay authoritative action twice.
- **Response rail:** step-by-step illumination synced to the existing `Game.response_step`/finish sequence; animation never advances rules independently.
- **Reduced motion:** disable camera shake, persistent flicker and excessive movement; retain static highlights, status updates and accessible cues.
- **Audio:** period electrical relay/button/CRT cues can be designed later; avoid duplicating effects on refresh.

Exact durations, audio assets and shader parameters require live Godot testing and review; no unverified timing is specified as canonical.

## 10. First vertical slice — visual and functional definition of done
**Deliverable is a launchable, visually complete, playable encounter, not an empty wrapper or programmer-art prototype.**

Required:
- The complete recognizable dense console composition: full-width header, left CRT/meter rail, major ICE well, mechanical response strip, illustrated card tray, counters and END TURN.
- Reusable Godot component composition; art, text, hitboxes and Game state separated.
- At least one **real** encounter selected from the existing implementation, with actual ICE/card artwork corresponding to that encounter; full playable loop from entering through Corporation Response, choices, ICE interaction and completion as applicable.
- Mouse and keyboard interactions, drag/drop, target previews, selected glow, hover/inspection, installed program feedback, resource changes, response sequence, reduced motion and correct visible copy.
- Responsive screenshot verification at target reference size and existing project viewport, plus smaller and larger landscape sizes.
- Deterministic comparison to existing prototype for gameplay outcomes; existing tests preserved, active CORE_LOOP screenshot coverage added; no false claim of Windows playtest.
- Designer review of **both** visual fidelity and gameplay parity. Do not substitute one for the other.

**Proposed visual review method:** render a 1536×864 Godot screenshot and compare side-by-side with the source image using matching crop, alignment and region labels. Review (a) overall silhouette, (b) region proportions, (c) material/render quality, (d) type readability, (e) card/ICE personality, (f) interaction states. Suggested rubric: 0=missing, 1=weak, 2=close, 3=faithful for each; **no automatic pass threshold or designer approval inferred**. Document intentional deviations (dynamic ICE count, real card text, accessibility, 1600×1000 adaptation).

## 11. Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Mockup uses illustrative/noncanonical card and ICE content | Bind actual Game/CardDB/IceDB; separate art from dynamic text |
| Dense art obstructs input/readability | Native controls, clear hitboxes, accessible focus, 4.5:1 contrast, optional inspected detail |
| Different aspect ratio than current project | Reference-space layout, controlled reflow, multiple viewport screenshots |
| 1/3 ICE vs two-window composition | Adaptive ICELane with documented variants and test screenshots |
| Giant flattened image prevents reusable components | Modular asset production, nine-slice, live labels and input |
| UI refresh invalidates drag or animations | Preserve CAS-001 identity/lifecycle and test parity |
| Effects leak hidden subroutines/copy | Filter visible data before rendering; existing SIGNAL-001 guards |
| GPU-heavy CRT/glow on GL Compatibility | Profile shader complexity and offer reduced effects |
| Artwork created before encounter selection | Confirm actual encounter/card roster before final asset batch |
| Reference JPEG inaccessible to Claude Code | Version exact original asset in repo with provenance before implementation |
